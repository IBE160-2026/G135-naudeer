# Product Brief: TrollaDel

## Executive Summary

TrollaDel is a proposed neighbourhood lending website that helps adults find and borrow everyday items from people nearby. The first version focuses on one complete journey: list an item, request a loan for specific dates, accept or decline the request, and confirm its return. It is a responsive website, not a native mobile application, and does not aim to replace activity-management services or become a general social network.

The problem hypothesis is that borrowing locally can require repeated messages and personal introductions because people do not know what is available or whether an item is free when they need it. Owners may be willing to lend but need clear dates, an identifiable borrower and an understandable record of the agreement. TrollaDel would make that coordination easier without handling payments.

Trolla is the proposed first test area. The project owner reports having local connections that may help recruit testers; no recruitment, interviews, completed loans or demand have yet been demonstrated. The immediate opportunity is to use the IBE160 project to build and test a small, useful service before deciding whether a wider business is justified.

## The Problem

A resident who needs an item briefly can buy it, rent it, ask acquaintances or post in an existing community channel. These alternatives can work well. The hypothesis to test is that some occasional needs remain inconvenient because information about availability, dates and return arrangements is scattered or unavailable to people outside established networks.

For example, someone preparing a small event might need an extra folding table for a weekend. A nearby owner might have one but not see the request, or might hesitate because the collection and return dates are unclear. This is an illustrative scenario, not a recorded user interview.

The potential consequences are unnecessary purchases, repeated coordination and missed lending opportunities. Their frequency and importance must be established through interviews and observed use, rather than assumed from general support for sharing.

## The Solution

An adult resident creates an account and lists an item with a description, condition, photo and approximate neighbourhood. Another resident searches or browses, selects collection and return dates, and sends a request. The owner accepts or declines. Acceptance establishes an agreed period; overlapping accepted loans for that item are blocked.

Both participants can see the request status and agreed dates. They arrange the handover privately using contact information shared only with the other participant after acceptance. The owner records collection and confirms return after checking the item; the borrower can indicate that it has been returned. An overdue loan remains visible rather than being silently treated as complete. Precise home addresses are not displayed in public listings.

The service coordinates lending; it does not guarantee identity, item safety, compensation or insurance. Early testing should use low-value, low-risk items and willing adult participants. Real-world testing begins only after the basic access controls and lending workflow have been checked.

## What Makes This Different

TrollaDel's proposed focus is free neighbourhood lending through a clear request-and-return workflow. It is narrower than a general community feed and is not intended to compete by offering the largest catalogue. Existing rental services and community groups are alternatives; this idea is not claimed to be unique.

The project owner's local connections offer a possible route to feedback and an initial supply of items. This is a recruitment opportunity, not a proven competitive advantage. Whether residents prefer a separate website over their current methods is a central validation question. There is no established technical moat or evidence of a scalable business yet.

## Who This Serves

The primary users are adults in the pilot neighbourhood who occasionally need an item and adults willing to lend suitable possessions. Borrowers need to find something relevant and obtain a clear answer. Owners need control over requests and confidence that the dates and return status are understood.

A pilot administrator needs to remove unsuitable listings, handle reports and support participants. Residents do not need to have children in organised activities or belong to a particular club. Trolla IL is not presented as a partner or sponsor: no such agreement has been made.

## Success Criteria

The following are proposed evaluation targets, not achieved results or evidence-based market thresholds:

- **Functional quality:** demonstrate and document the full listing, request, approval, collection and return journey, plus decline and cancellation. Automated tests must check that overlapping accepted loans are rejected and that a user cannot change another owner's listings or decisions.
- **Usability:** invite five adult testers; aim for at least four to complete the core task without step-by-step assistance. Record difficulties as well as successful attempts. If recruitment is lower, report the actual number and the limitation.
- **Pilot usefulness:** aim for ten volunteer participants, fifteen suitable listings and five completed real loans during a four-week pilot after the prototype is ready. Look for at least two participants to start another loan without an individual reminder. These pilot targets are not additional course deadlines.
- **Learning and business decision:** document at least five conversations about recent borrowing needs, alternatives and concerns. Continue investment only after reviewing actual loan activity, repeat use, coordination effort and reasons for non-use. Free use does not establish willingness to pay.

## Scope

The first version includes account access, simple profiles, item listings, basic search/filtering, dated loan requests, owner approval or rejection, cancellation, collection and return statuses, and minimal reporting/removal tools. Its priority is a reliable end-to-end lending flow on desktop and mobile browsers. Only the owner and the requesting borrower can access the private details of their loan.

The pilot is limited to adults, one local area and low-value, low-risk items. Before inviting real users, the project must define acceptable items, consent for testing, necessary personal data, retention/deletion procedures and how disputes or overdue items are handled. Collect only information needed for the pilot; do not claim that an account is identity-verified.

The first version excludes payments, deposits, insurance, identity-document collection, public home addresses, live chat, public ratings, delivery services, native mobile apps, multiple launch regions, recommendation algorithms and a social-media feed. Commercialisation, formal partnerships and a wider launch are later decisions. If time is short, reduce discovery features rather than remove access-control checks or the core lending journey.

## Vision

If the pilot demonstrates useful, repeatable lending, TrollaDel could develop over two to three years into a service for several small communities. Expansion would be staged, checking whether each new area can attract both lenders and borrowers without depending on the founder's personal involvement.

Possible organisational customers and sustainable revenue models would be investigated only after the core service proves useful. Partnerships, payment handling or compensation arrangements would require separate validation and appropriate review. The long-term ambition is a viable product that helps communities use existing resources better; the near-term commitment is a small, testable website and an honest account of what works and what does not.
